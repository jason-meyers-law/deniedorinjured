# PLAN.md — DeniedOrInjured.com

## 1. Project Overview

Build `DeniedOrInjured.com` as the primary website and long-term digital authority for a Florida law practice focused on:

1. Personal injury claims for injured people.
2. Insurance coverage disputes involving insurers that deny a defense, deny indemnity, reserve rights, or mishandle settlement opportunities.
3. Florida bad-faith and failure-to-settle matters.

The website should launch with **personal injury as the dominant topical authority**. The insurance-denial / coverage / bad-faith silo should be reserved in the architecture from day one but expanded after the personal-injury foundation is established.

The website must be designed for both:

- Traditional search engines such as Google and Bing.
- AI-assisted search and answer engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, and future LLM-based retrieval systems.

Do **not** approach this as a collection of generic SEO landing pages. Build it as a structured legal knowledge base authored and reviewed by a real Florida lawyer, with strong local/entity signals, first-hand legal experience, primary-source citations, and clear answers to questions injured people actually ask.

No ranking or citation position can be guaranteed.

# 4. Strategic Principles

## 4.1 One authoritative website

Do not build separate “SEO” and “AI” websites.

Use one canonical domain with:

- Strong information architecture.
- Original legal content.
- Real author identities.
- Primary sources.
- Accurate structured data.
- Internal linking.
- Fast crawlable HTML.
- Clear business identity.
- Strong local signals.

Avoid duplicating substantially similar personal-injury content across `JasonMeyersLaw.com` and `DeniedOrInjured.com`.

A migration / redirect strategy must be created for any overlapping content.

## 4.2 Personal injury first

At launch, most substantive authority should be in the personal-injury silo.

The insurance-coverage / denied-defense silo should have URL space reserved but should not be populated with thin “coming soon” content.

## 4.3 Answer real questions

Content should be driven by genuine client questions and legal decision points, not keyword variations.

Do not create separate pages for trivial keyword variants such as:

- “car accident attorney Melbourne”
- “Melbourne auto accident lawyer”
- “Melbourne car crash attorney”

unless the pages serve genuinely different user intent.

## 4.4 Original expertise over generic copy

Every major article should contain something that demonstrates actual legal knowledge or first-hand professional experience, such as:

- What insurers routinely request.
- What adjusters investigate.
- What defense lawyers look for.
- What evidence changes claim evaluation.
- What mistakes reduce settlement value.
- What happens procedurally after suit is filed.
- What actually occurs in depositions, mediation, and trial.
- How Florida insurance law interacts with injury claims.

Do not fabricate anecdotes, results, statistics, or “insider” observations.

## 4.5 Cite primary authority

Prefer:

1. Florida Statutes.
2. Florida Supreme Court and District Court of Appeal opinions.
3. Florida Rules of Civil Procedure.
4. Florida Standard Jury Instructions.
5. Official Florida agency materials.
6. Federal statutes/regulations where relevant.
7. Government medical/safety resources.
8. Reputable secondary authorities only where appropriate.

Each legal article should identify the date it was last legally reviewed.

# 7. Standard Article Template

Every substantive legal answer should follow this structure.

## H1

Use the natural-language question or topic.

Example:

`Should I Give the Insurance Company a Recorded Statement After a Florida Car Accident?`

## Direct answer

Open with a concise 2–4 sentence answer.

The answer should stand alone and make sense if extracted by an AI system.

Do not begin with:

- “It depends.”
- A long biography.
- A marketing pitch.
- A history lesson.
- Keyword stuffing.

If the answer depends on facts, say what the main dependency is.

## Action checklist

Provide the immediate practical steps the reader should consider.

## Florida legal rule

Explain the governing legal framework in plain English.

Cite primary authority.

## Important exceptions

Explain what could change the answer.

## Evidence / documentation

Explain what evidence may matter.

## Insurance-company perspective

Where truthful and relevant, explain what an insurer, adjuster, or defense lawyer may examine.

This should draw on real professional experience, not generic speculation.

## Example

Use a hypothetical or clearly identified de-identified example.

Never imply that a hypothetical is an actual client result.

## What happens next

Explain process and expected sequence.

## When to speak with a lawyer

Explain when legal assistance becomes important without claiming every reader needs representation.

## Related questions

Include contextual internal links.

## Sources

Provide authoritative citations.

## Author block

Include:

- Jason O. Meyers.
- Florida attorney.
- Relevant experience.
- Link to full attorney biography.
- Original publication date.
- Last legal review date.

## CTA

Use a restrained, relevant CTA.

Example concept:

> If you were seriously injured in Brevard County or elsewhere in Florida and want to discuss how the insurance issues affect your claim, contact Jason Meyers Law, PLLC.

Final wording must be reviewed for legal-advertising compliance.

# 8. Content Style Guide

## Voice

Use:

- Plain English.
- Calm authority.
- Direct answers.
- Specific examples.
- Florida-specific terminology.
- Short paragraphs.
- Descriptive headings.
- Practical guidance.

Avoid:

- “In today’s complex legal landscape…”
- “Navigating the aftermath…”
- “You may be entitled to compensation.”
- Excessive “fighting for you” clichés.
- Fake urgency.
- “Millions recovered” unless verified, compliant, and contextualized.
- AI filler.
- Generic definitions copied from other law-firm sites.

## Reading level

Write for a smart non-lawyer.

Legal terms should be used accurately and then explained.

## Length

Do not target arbitrary word counts.

A page should be as long as needed to fully answer the intent.

Typical ranges:

- Narrow FAQ: 700–1,500 words.
- Major answer page: 1,200–2,500 words.
- Core practice page: 1,500–3,000+ words.
- Location page: only as long as genuine local content justifies.

Do not inflate content for SEO.
