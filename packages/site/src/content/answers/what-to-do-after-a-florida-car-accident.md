## Action checklist

1. Move to safety if you can; call 911 and ask for law enforcement.
2. Get checked medically the same day if there is any chance of injury. [PENDING VERIFICATION — the PIP treatment-window rule and its consequences belong here once verified.]
3. Photograph vehicles, positions, debris, skid marks, and the wider scene before anything moves.
4. Get names and phone numbers of witnesses — they leave quickly.
5. Exchange license, registration, and insurance information.
6. Notify your own insurer of the crash.
7. Write down what happened while it is fresh.

## What to hold off on

- Recorded statements to the **other** driver's insurer.
- Broad medical-record authorizations.
- Quick settlement offers made before the injuries are understood.
- Posting about the crash or your activities on social media.
- Repairing or releasing your vehicle before it is photographed and, in a serious case, inspected.

## Why the first days matter to the claim

Insurers begin evaluating immediately: the adjuster's file starts with the crash report, the recorded statements, and the first medical records. Camera footage from nearby businesses is often overwritten within days. What is preserved — or lost — in the first week shapes everything after.
