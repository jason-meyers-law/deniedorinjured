## The two insurers are not the same

- **The at-fault driver's insurer** works for the other side. Its adjuster's job includes finding statements that reduce or defeat your claim. You owe it no recorded statement.
- **Your own insurer** owes you benefits (PIP, MedPay, UM/UIM) and your policy obliges you to cooperate. [PENDING ATTORNEY REVIEW — cooperation-clause consequences under current Florida law.]

## What adjusters listen for

Recorded statements are taken early on purpose, before you know the extent of your injuries. Common traps: "How are you today?" answered with a reflexive "fine"; estimating speeds and distances you did not actually measure; minimizing symptoms; guessing instead of saying "I don't know."

## If a statement is going to happen

Prepare first. Review what you actually know, decline to guess, and do not speculate about injuries that are still being diagnosed. For a UM/UIM or disputed-liability claim, have a lawyer involved before any recorded statement.
