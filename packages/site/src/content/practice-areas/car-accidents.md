A Florida car-accident claim is decided by three things: who was at fault, what the injuries are, and what insurance is actually available to pay. Most of the frustration injured people experience comes from the third one — Florida's no-fault system routes the first medical bills through your own policy, and the at-fault driver's coverage is often far smaller than the harm done.

## What insurance may apply

- **PIP (personal injury protection)** on your own policy — the no-fault first layer. [PENDING VERIFICATION — current Fla. Stat. § 627.736 limits and treatment-window rule.]
- **Bodily-injury liability** coverage on the at-fault driver's policy.
- **Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** coverage on your own policy, when the at-fault driver has no insurance or not enough.
- **MedPay and health insurance**, each with its own repayment rules.

## What evidence matters

The crash report, scene photos, witness contacts, the vehicles themselves, early medical records, and — increasingly — nearby camera footage that is routinely overwritten within days. Preserve first, argue later.

## Mistakes that reduce claim value

Gaps in treatment, recorded statements given without preparation, broad medical authorizations signed early, social-media posts, and repairing or releasing a vehicle before it is documented.

## What the process looks like

Investigation and treatment first, then a demand supported by records, then negotiation. If the insurer will not pay what the claim is worth, suit, discovery, depositions, mediation, and — for a small fraction of cases — trial.
